# AI Governance Policy — Synthetic Example

> **Fictional teaching sample.** The organisation, system, roles, use case, and
> controls below are invented. This is not a real policy, legal advice, or
> evidence of compliance. Adapt it with qualified owners before use.

- **Organisation:** Example Organisation
- **Policy owner:** AI Governance Lead
- **Approval authority:** Example Executive Committee
- **Review:** At least annually and after a material change

## 1. Purpose

This policy sets the principles and responsibilities for developing,
procuring, deploying, and operating AI systems on behalf of Example
Organisation. It applies across the system lifecycle, from initial proposal to
retirement.

## 2. Scope

This fictional example concerns an AI-assisted eligibility support service.
The service summarizes approved information and recommends cases for review;
an authorized employee makes the final eligibility decision.

The policy applies to employees, contractors, and suppliers who design,
configure, operate, assess, or provide services for an in-scope AI system.
Unrelated business software and systems outside the organisation's control
require separate scope decisions.

## 3. Governance principles

### 3.1 Defined purpose and boundaries

Each system must have an approved purpose, intended users, affected
stakeholders, operating boundaries, and a named business owner. Teams must
assess a proposed new use before enabling it.

### 3.2 Human accountability

People retain responsibility for decisions assigned to them. System owners
must specify when a person reviews an output, what information the reviewer
needs, and how the workflow behaves if review is unavailable.

### 3.3 Fair treatment

Before deployment, the responsible team defines relevant outcome measures,
data limitations, and review methods for potential differences among affected
groups. Qualified reviewers assess flagged results and record the disposition.

### 3.4 Privacy and security

Teams document data sources, permitted uses, access boundaries, retention, and
security controls before processing personal or sensitive information. They
use only data approved for the system's stated purpose.

### 3.5 Transparency and records

Users receive information appropriate to their role about AI involvement and
the limits of generated output. The system owner identifies which decisions,
versions, approvals, and material changes need records and protects those
records under approved access and retention rules.

### 3.6 Monitoring and improvement

Owners define how performance, incidents, user feedback, and material changes
will be reviewed. A signal outside an approved tolerance goes to a named owner
for investigation and a recorded decision.

## 4. Minimum lifecycle responsibilities

| Stage | Required decision or evidence | Accountable role |
|---|---|---|
| Proposal | Purpose, affected users, initial risk screen, and intended use | Business owner |
| Design | Data, system boundaries, human role, and control plan | System owner |
| Validation | Test plan, results, limitations, and unresolved findings | Validation lead |
| Approval | Recorded decision and any conditions before release | Authorized approver |
| Operation | Monitoring results, incidents, user feedback, and follow-up | System owner |
| Change or retirement | Impact review, transition plan, record retention, and closure | Business owner |

The organisation must define separation between the person preparing a
high-impact change and the person approving it. A person must not approve
outside their assigned authority.

## 5. Exceptions and concerns

The system owner records a policy exception with its scope, rationale,
affected controls, risk decision, approver, expiry or review trigger, and
compensating measures. An exception does not replace required legal,
regulatory, or contractual review.

Staff and affected users must have a documented route to report a concern.
The assigned owner records the report, triage, decision, and any corrective
action in an access-controlled system.

## 6. Review and approval

The policy owner reviews this policy on its scheduled cadence and after a
material change to the organisation, applicable obligations, or AI system
portfolio. The approving authority records approval and the effective version.

## 7. Adoption checklist

Before adopting this sample:

1. Replace the fictional scope with the approved system inventory and intended
   uses.
2. Confirm applicable obligations and define who can approve each decision.
3. Set evidence, monitoring, retention, and escalation rules that match the
   system and organisation.
4. Obtain required reviews and approvals; publish the controlled version.
